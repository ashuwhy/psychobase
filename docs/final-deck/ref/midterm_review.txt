                                    Research Objective

  Can a Chatbot That Knows What Your Body Is Doing
                 Support You Better?
Standard emotional-support chatbots respond only to what a person types — they have no way to
notice that someone is tense, worn down, or escalating before they say so in words. This project
asks whether giving a conversational model access to a person's real-time physiological state,
not just their message, produces replies that are more attuned, more appropriately timed, and
more consistent with how that person is actually doing.

To test this, we paired a wearable sensor stream — electrodermal activity, pulse rate, skin
temperature and movement — with real conversations, and fine-tuned a compact language model
(SmolLM2-1.7B) to generate a supportive reply from both the user's words and a short
physiological summary. We then compared the model's behaviour with and without that signal,
and evaluated its responses, automatically and by hand, against the text-only alternative.

Results · Data Formatting Lane


Does Training Order Matter?
 Arrangement                                 Eval Loss                    Finding

 Interleaved (baseline)                      2.075                        Case 1 and case 2 for a turn sit back to back — lowest loss of the
                                                                          three

 Randomised                                  2.134                        No positional pattern at all — the cleanest control, but a weaker
                                                                          physio signal

 Batched                                     2.191                        All case 1s, then all case 2s — highest loss, yet still beats the
                                                                          majority baseline


In practice — a turn's two training strings are Case 1 (message only) and Case 2 (message + physio summary): Interleaved = T1‑C1, T1‑C2, T2‑C1, T2‑C2… ·
Batched = all C1s, then all C2s · Randomised = T340‑C2, T12‑C1, T88‑C2…

Same model, same method — only the order of the 1,240 training strings changes. Interleaved wins, and the feared "batched learns a shortcut" risk
checked out unfounded against the generations.
